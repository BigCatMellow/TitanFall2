A stall flag means less than 50 units of horizontal displacement over a
roughly 10-second window while pursuing/recovering/embarking. These are
sample counts, not 254 separate stuck events or 10% of whole match time.
The general exporter also includes Titan stalls (293 total Eden flags);
this review filters to pilots for evaluating the new pilot behavior.

211/254 Eden pilot stall samples (83.1%) were in the small area
x4000..4300,y-450..-100, concentrated around (4120,-272,120..128).
Six bots in slots 2, 4, 6, 8, 12, 14 repeatedly encountered it. All these samples
were team 2, the human's team in this round. Split: 146 pursue, 43 recover,
22 embark. They were not intentional range holds. One bot's consecutive
flagged sequence spans 16 seconds after already meeting the stall threshold.
Eden also logged approximately 52,164 path requests versus 4,062/3,646 in the
Meadow rounds; durations differ, so these totals alone are not a fair rate
comparison. 331 no-progress recoveries and 10 waypoint timeouts were logged

Proposed next navigation work: reproduce this exact location and inspect
start/goal/empty-path handling and Titan boarding access. Consider bounded
retry delays or a verified nearby route alternative. Do not resurrect the
rejected v14 goal blacklists/caches or globally relax navigation because
Meadow's movement looks much healthier. Logs do not contain terrain geometry
and do not prove the specific obstruction or cause.

### 2. Targets are changing back and forth too quickly

From actual BOT_DECISION events, counting an immediate A->B then B->A
reversal within 0.5 seconds:

| Round | Logged changes | Quick switch-backs |
|---|---:|---:|
| Eden |2,162|622 (28.8%)|
| Meadow1 |2,216|719 (32.4%)|
| Meadow2 |2,582|750 (29.0%)|

Across Meadow, 1,469/4,798 changes (30.6%) quickly reversed. These are
reversal events, not necessarily distinct episodes. For example, epoch 3
slot 1 swapped at 59.58, 59.62 and 59.65 seconds, with engage reasons each time.
This is strong evidence of target churn, not proof that each switch was
wrong. Two engage reasons do not prove both enemies remained simultaneously
visible; LOS flicker, urgent threats, deaths and changing distances can
justify a change.

Proposed small combat trial: 150–250ms of extra commitment to the current
valid, visible target, with explicit close-threat/death/LOS-loss overrides.
The existing coordinator's 1.5-second preference is bypassed for threats
within 400 units; do not simply increase all commitment times. Add reason
telemetry for abandoning a target so visibility flicker can be distinguished
from nearest-threat oscillation. Keep aim_error_scale 0.78.

### 3. Some close-range pauses deserve firing/step diagnostics

On Eden, slots 7/8 remained nearly stationary across 65.02..77.02 seconds,
roughly 187 units apart for most of it. Both were in range_hold, with clips
unchanged at 15/13 in the two-second samples; blocked-step counters increased
from 14/30 to 74/91. A sampled command included ADS/walk but no attack.
This suggests a firing/positioning hesitation worth reproducing, rather than
assuming all low movement is intended. The current records cannot prove
why attack was withheld or that every intervening frame lacked a shot.

Code review: a moment without a shootable target restarts the 0.8-second
reaction timer; unavailable offhand inputs can also suppress primary attack.
Either could contribute, but neither is established as this episode's cause.
Next instrumentation should record no-fire reason, reaction timer, actual
shot deltas, and whether a step failed due to ground, wall or airborne state.
Then consider a tiny LOS reacquisition grace or an independently checked
opposite sidestep. Preserve wall/ledge checks; the opposite side may also
be blocked, and static firing from cover can be legitimate.
